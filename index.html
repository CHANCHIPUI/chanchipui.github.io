MarvisNav: Making Memory Visible on Route Choices for Zero-Shot Object Navigation

Jincheng Wang, Chi Pui Chan, Wei Zeng, Shuyang Zhang, Jianhao Jiao*, Dimitrios Kanoulas

A zero-shot object navigation framework that draws topological exploration memory directly onto the egocentric route choices a VLM selects from. Tested on a Unitree Go2, indoors and out.

  1. 1

    Keep a state-aware graph

    The robot maps free space and skeletonises it into a reduced Voronoi graph. Junctions are where it decides, and every node carries one of four exploration states.

  2. 2

    Draw it on the view

    At a junction, visible paths and candidate nodes are projected into a few egocentric views. Each candidate gets a letter and the colour of its state.

  3. 3

    Let the VLM choose

    One VLM query weighs target relevance against what is left to explore and answers with a letter, which becomes the planner's next goal.

Real-Time Person Following on a Mobile Robot

A YOLO detector and an OSNet re-identification network keep the robot locked on its target person. An Extended Kalman Filter fuses vision with odometry to keep tracking through occlusions, and an artificial potential field planner pulls the robot toward the person and away from obstacles. Deployed in ROS 2.
